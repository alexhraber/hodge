# Next research obligation: beyond multiplicity-one symmetry

The complete-intersection calculation proves that one nonzero coefficient per one-dimensional character space is enough on the Fermat variety, since a diagonal automorphism projector isolates it. On a general smooth projective variety, a rational Hodge class need not belong to a one-dimensional eigenspace of any algebraic automorphism group.

A useful abstraction is the following conditional criterion. For a smooth projective fourfold, let $V$ be its primitive rational Hodge space. Suppose a rational algebra $B$ of algebraic correspondences acts on $V$, preserving algebraicity, and is semisimple on $V$. For each simple $B$-module type, write the isotypic component as a direct sum of copies of that simple module. The orbit of an algebraic class under $B$ can span at most the submodule it generates; to span $V$, the module generated by the supplied algebraic seeds must have full multiplicity in each isotypic component. A nonzero projection into each isotypic component suffices only when those components are simple. In general a rank condition in the multiplicity spaces is indispensable. No semisimple correspondence algebra with these properties is known universally or assumed here.

The Fermat calculation realizes this criterion with the rational group algebra and the diagonal Galois-orbit blocks (each appears with multiplicity one). The next experiment should test explicit dominant rational maps and algebraic correspondences obtained by splitting Fermat polynomials, generate their induced character support, and compare to residual sets for degrees 8, 9, 10, 12, 18, 20. A promising direction is Shioda's inductive decomposition via products of lower-dimensional Fermat varieties, not a claim that automorphism projectors exist on general varieties.

To extend to arbitrary smooth projective fourfolds, prove a geometric construction producing full multiplicity of algebraic classes or a universally nonzero detector as in R3. The correspondence criterion merely identifies the exact missing condition; it is not a solution.
